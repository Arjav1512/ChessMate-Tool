# Improving 1200–1800

In this range pieces mostly stop hanging in one move — games are now decided by two-to-three-move tactics, planless middlegames, and butchered endgames. The recipe broadens: pattern volume, plan discipline, endgame technique.

**Why it matters:** This is where most players plateau — usually because their study menu still looks like a beginner's (more openings) or a master's (deep strategy) instead of what actually decides THEIR games.

**Recognize it:** You're in this band's failure modes when: you reach fine middlegames and drift ("I developed... now what?"), you lose won positions in endings, tactics puzzles go fine but in games you miss the same motifs, and your opening study keeps growing while your results don't.

**Common mistakes:**
- Opening maximalism: knowing twelve systems five moves deep instead of two systems with their middlegame PLANS.
- Tactics volume without pattern classification — a thousand random puzzles teach less than three hundred sorted by motif (pin week, deflection week...).
- Endgame procrastination: rook endings are a quarter of your games and probably a tenth of your study.

**How to think:** Structure every game around a stated plan (write it down at move 15 in study games: "minority attack," "trade into the good-knight ending"). Adopt structured calculation: candidates first, forcing moves checked every move, blunder-check before releasing. Build an endgame core: Lucena, Philidor, key K+P positions, opposition — then TRUST simplification as a winning technique when ahead.

**Example:** A typical 1500 loss: fine Carlsbad middlegame, no plan, twenty shuffling moves, then an equal rook ending misplayed for the full point. The player "lost to a stronger opponent" — actually lost to two trainable gaps: not knowing the minority attack existed, and not knowing Philidor's position held trivially.

**Coach's tip:** Analyze YOUR OWN games more than you study anything else — every loss sorted into: tactic missed (which motif?), plan absent (which structure?), or endgame botched (which position?). Your games are a syllabus written specifically for you.

**Train it:** Weekly rhythm: 3 slow games, 30 motif-sorted puzzles, one endgame position mastered, one full self-analysis before engine-checking it. Sustainable, complete, and plateau-breaking.
