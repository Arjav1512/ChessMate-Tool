# Opposition

Opposition is the king-versus-king tempo duel: kings face each other an odd number of squares apart, and whoever must move first loses ground. It decides who penetrates — and therefore who wins most pawn endings.

**Why it matters:** In king-and-pawn endings, opposition IS the evaluation: the same position wins or draws depending purely on whose turn it is. No other concept flips as many half-points.

**Recognize it:** Direct opposition: kings on the same line, one square apart — the side NOT to move "has" it. Distant opposition: same line, any odd number of squares — convertible into direct by advancing. Diagonal opposition guards two approach routes at once. In all forms the question is the same: who can force the other king to step aside?

**Common mistakes:**
- Grabbing opposition when key squares — not opposition — decide (opposition is the TOOL; reaching the key squares is the GOAL: if you can reach one directly, ignore the duel).
- Wasting reserve pawn moves early: each spare tempo (a2-a3 in the bank) lets you seize opposition on demand — spend them last.
- Attacker exchanging into "won" positions without checking WHO has the move — count it explicitly, every time.

**How to think:** Work goal-first: identify the key squares your king must reach (for a pawn on e4: d6/e6/f6). Then ask whether opposition wins the entry fight: take direct opposition when the kings meet; hold distant opposition when they haven't; break ties with saved pawn tempi. Defender's mirror: hold opposition to deny every key square, and aim your king for the queening corner.

**Example:** Kings on e4 and e6, White pawn on e3, White to move: only king moves exist, so White must yield — 1.Kd4 Kd6! 2.e4 Ke6 3.e5 Ke7! 4.Kd5 Kd7 and Black concedes nothing; draw (9 moves later it is stalemate). Put the pawn on e2 instead and 1.e3! spends the spare tempo, hands Black the move, and White's king walks in and wins. Same squares, one reserve tempo, opposite result.

**Coach's tip:** In any pawn-ending calculation, literally say "…and now it is HIS move" at the critical face-off. Making the tempo explicit catches the miscounts that silent visualization slips past.

**Train it:** Drill the K+P vs K matrix until every position is instant: pawn on each rank, kings everywhere, both sides to move. It's an afternoon of work that lasts a lifetime.
