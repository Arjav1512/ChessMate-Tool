# Queen Endgames

Queen endings are perpetual-check minefields where passed pawns outrank everything: an advanced passer with the queen behind it beats an extra pawn — sometimes two — because checks decide what material cannot.

**Why it matters:** They're the endings players know least and misplay most: winning positions dissolve into perpetual check, and "drawn" ones are lost to one careless king walk. A few rules restore order in the chaos.

**Recognize it:** The hierarchy inverts: (1) advanced passed pawn — the single dominant factor; (2) king safety — can YOUR king escape checks? (a king with shelter is a winning advantage by itself); (3) queen activity/centralization; (4) only then, pawn count.

**Common mistakes:**
- Counting pawns instead of counting the passer's rank — Q+5 vs Q+4 with their runner on b2 is LOSING, not winning.
- Walking the king into the open where perpetual check lives: king moves in queen endings are calculated like tactics, always.
- Checking aimlessly as the defender instead of building the perpetual NET (checks must chain — one non-check defensive queen move usually means the perpetual is gone).
- Refusing the queen trade one move too long — every queen ending should be played with the resulting pawn ending pre-calculated.

**How to think:** Attacker's doctrine: escort your passer with the queen from BEHIND, march your king toward shelter (often the enemy's pawn cover!), centralize to cut check-lines, and offer queen trades whenever the pawn ending wins. Defender's doctrine: checks first, checks always — the moment checks run dry, hunt the passer; place your own passer as far and fast as possible (counterplay outranks material here more than anywhere).

**Example:** The standard win: queen on b8 behind a b6-passer versus checks. The winning king doesn't hide at home — it WALKS, check by check, across the board toward the b-pawn or into the defender's own pawn shield, where checks end forever. The technique is a king journey, not a pawn push.

**Coach's tip:** Before any move in a queen ending ask two questions in order: "does this allow perpetual?" and "does this help my passer?" A move failing both is a mistake even when it wins material.

**Train it:** Play Q+passer-on-6th versus Q positions from both sides against an engine. Learning to WALK the king through checks once makes every future queen ending calmer.
