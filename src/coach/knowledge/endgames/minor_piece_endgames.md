# Minor Piece Endgames

Bishop and knight endings turn on piece-nature: bishops rule open, two-wing positions; knights rule closed, one-wing ones. Same material, wildly different games — knowing which animal wins where is the skill.

**Why it matters:** Every minor-piece trade in the middlegame is secretly choosing one of these endings. The classic conversions — good-vs-bad bishop, opposite-colored-bishop draws, outside-passer-vs-knight — decide games long before move 60.

**Recognize it:** The four families: same-colored bishops (good-vs-bad bishop battles — whose pawns block whose bishop?), opposite-colored bishops (the great drawing force: even two pawns up often can't win — but with OTHER pieces on, the same imbalance ATTACKS), bishop vs knight (open board + two wings = bishop; blocked + one wing = knight), knight vs knight (a pure tempo-and-outpost war — an outside passer is huge because knights defend distant threats terribly).

**Common mistakes:**
- Fixing your own pawns on your bishop's color — the "bad bishop" is self-inflicted in one careless pawn move ("pawns on the opposite color of YOUR bishop").
- Trading into opposite-colored bishops when ahead (instant drawing chances for them) — or NOT trading into them when worse (the escape hatch).
- Racing a knight against a wing passer — knights lose to distant passers; bishops laugh at them.

**How to think:** Before minor-piece trades ask: which family results, and does my pawn structure fit my remaining piece? In the ending itself: put pawns opposite your bishop's color, create play on BOTH wings with a bishop (stretch the knight), keep it on ONE wing with a knight, and remember zugzwang — bishops have waiting moves, knights don't ("the knight runs out of moves first").

**Example:** Good-vs-bad bishop archetype: French-structure ending, White Bd3 vs Black Bc8 behind e6/d5 pawns. White fixes more pawns on light squares (a4, h5), walks the king in via the dark squares the bishop can never guard, and Black's bishop dies of its own pawns. The game was decided by pawn placements made twenty moves earlier.

**Coach's tip:** With a knight versus a bishop, your job is a fixed, closed structure and one great outpost. With the bishop, your job is open diagonals and pawns racing on both wings — play the piece's nature, not the material count.

**Train it:** Play the same 3-vs-3-plus-passer position four times: B vs B same color, opposite color, B vs N, N vs N. One evening, the whole taxonomy in your hands.
