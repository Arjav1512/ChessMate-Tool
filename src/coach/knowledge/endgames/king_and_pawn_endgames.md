# King and Pawn Endgames

Pure pawn endings are chess reduced to arithmetic: no rescues, no swindles — every position is a forced win, loss, or draw, and one tempo separates them. They must be calculated, never judged.

**Why it matters:** Every trade decision in every simpler endgame implicitly asks "what if we end up in the pawn ending?" Whoever calculates pawn endings correctly owns every trade negotiation before it happens.

**Recognize it:** Only kings and pawns remain. The three governing tools: the rule of the square (a king catches a passer if it stands inside the square drawn from pawn to promotion), key squares (for a lone pawn: the three squares two ranks ahead — king reaches one, promotion is forced), and opposition (kings facing with one square between — whoever must move yields ground).

**Common mistakes:**
- Trading into a pawn ending because you're "a pawn up" — many extra-pawn endings are dead draws (wrong-side passed pawn, blocked majorities, rook pawns).
- Moving the pawn before the king: the king leads, the pawn follows — pushing early burns the tempi that opposition battles are decided by.
- Forgetting spare pawn tempi: a saved a2-a3 move flips who has opposition; count reserve moves BEFORE the kings lock.

**How to think:** Calculate to promotion or proven draw — always. Procedure: identify the critical passer or breakthrough, apply the square rule for races, fight for key squares with opposition when kings duel, and use pawn tempi as the tiebreaker. Distant candidates: "outside passed pawn wins" — it deflects their king while yours harvests the other wing.

**Example:** White Ke5 and Pe4 versus Black Ke7 — king one square in front of its pawn, so the opposition decides everything. White to move: 1.Kd5 Kd7! 2.e5 Ke7 3.e6 Ke8! 4.Kd6 Kd8 5.e7+ Ke8 6.Ke6 stalemate — draw. Black to move: 1...Kd7 2.Kf6! and the outflanked king cannot stop the pawn. One tempo, entire result. (And remember the stronger rule: with the king on the SIXTH in front of the pawn, White wins regardless of the move.)

**Coach's tip:** Before ANY final piece trade, freeze: count the resulting pawn ending to the end — squares, opposition, tempi. "Probably winning" is not an evaluation in pawn endings; it's a coin flip.

**Train it:** Master five canonical positions cold (K+P vs K all cases, the e5/e6/e8 opposition duel, outside-passer wins, a breakthrough motif) — then drill mixed K+P puzzles weekly.
