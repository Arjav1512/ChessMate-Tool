# Endgame Principles

The endgame reverses middlegame instincts: kings attack, pawns become the point of everything, and one tempo routinely decides. General principles guide — but concrete calculation rules.

**Why it matters:** Endgames are where advantages become points and where most club players donate half-points weekly. Technique here is the cheapest rating available: the theory is finite and the positions repeat.

**Recognize it:** You're in an endgame when kings can safely join the fight — usually queens off and limited attacking force. The evaluation currency changes instantly: activity and passed pawns outweigh raw material more than anywhere else.

**Common mistakes:**
- Leaving the king home three moves too long — the king is a fighting piece worth ~3 points; centralize it FIRST.
- Pushing passed pawns prematurely ("passed pawns must be pushed" — but only with support; alone they get blockaded and lost).
- Trading into pawn endgames on general feeling: pawn endings are concrete like tactics — calculate to the end or don't enter.
- Hurrying: endgames reward the side that improves everything before committing ("do not hurry" is technique, not cowardice).

**How to think:** Endgame priority list: (1) king activity — centralize before any plan; (2) passed pawn creation — your pawn majority exists to produce one ("candidate forward first"); (3) rook/piece activity — an active rook outweighs a pawn; (4) opposition and key squares when kings face off; (5) count tempi in races — endgames are arithmetic, not art.

**Example:** The fundamental race question: K+P endings hinge on the rule of the square (can the king catch the runner?) and opposition (who must yield front squares). A position with even material where one king stands two squares more central is usually simply winning — that's the endgame exchange rate for activity.

**Coach's tip:** Learn exact positions, not just principles: Lucena, Philidor, key-square K+P wins, the wrong-bishop draw. Ten memorized positions anchor a thousand practical decisions.

**Train it:** Play K+P endings against an engine from random level starting points, 10 minutes daily for two weeks. Nothing improves practical results faster per hour spent.
