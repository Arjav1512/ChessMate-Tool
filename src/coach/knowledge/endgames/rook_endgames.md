# Rook Endgames

Rook endings are the most common endgame family (roughly half of all endgames) and the most drawish — activity routinely outweighs a pawn, and technique outweighs both.

**Why it matters:** "All rook endgames are drawn" is a joke with teeth: extra pawns evaporate against active defense, and passive defense loses drawn positions. Nowhere does knowledge convert so directly into half-points.

**Recognize it:** The governing laws: rooks belong BEHIND passed pawns (yours or theirs); an active rook is worth a pawn; the defending king wants the short side, the checking rook the long side; cutting the enemy king off (by file or rank) is often the whole game.

**Common mistakes:**
- Passive rook defense (rook glued in front of a passer or babysitting a pawn) — the losing setup in thousands of games; grab activity even at material cost.
- Not knowing the two pillars cold: Philidor's draw (rook on the 6th until the pawn advances, then check from behind forever) and Lucena's win (king to the 8th, "build the bridge" with the rook on the 4th).
- Racing pawns without first cutting off the enemy king — one file of cut-off equals about one tempo, and rook endings are decided by tempi.

**How to think:** Evaluation checklist: rook activity (behind passers? 7th rank?), king activity and cut-offs, passed-pawn quality (outside passers deflect; connected passers win), then material. Defender's doctrine: activate the rook FIRST, harass from behind/long side, aim for known drawn setups (Philidor, rook-pawn fortresses). Attacker's doctrine: fix the defender in passivity before advancing anything.

**Example:** Lucena, the winning method every player must own: White pawn e7, king e8, Black checking from behind. Build the bridge: slide the rook to the fourth rank (Re1–e4), step the king out with Kd7, and when the checks begin walk it down — Kd6, Kd5 — toward the rook, until the rook on e4 finally blocks the last check itself. The checks run out, the pawn queens. Five moves of technique, memorized once, converts every such position forever.

**Coach's tip:** In rook endings your first question is never "how many pawns?" but "whose rook is happier?" Trade a pawn for rook activity without regret; refuse an extra pawn that costs your rook its life.

**Train it:** Master Lucena and Philidor until you can play both blindfolded, then play R+4 vs R+3 same-side against an engine monthly — the single most practical endgame drill there is.
