# Blunder Prevention

A blunder is a move that throws away the game's evaluation in one stroke. Prevention is not talent — it is a repeatable pre-move routine performed every single move.

**Why it matters:** One blunder outweighs forty good moves. Cutting your blunder rate in half raises your rating more than any amount of new knowledge, because it applies to every game you'll ever play.

**Recognize it:** Know YOUR trigger conditions — they're predictable: low clock, move 10-15 "autopilot" zone, just-won-material relaxation, long-think tunnel vision (deep calculation of one idea, zero seconds on the reply), and emotional tilt after an earlier mistake.

**Common mistakes:**
- Running the safety check only in "dangerous" positions — blunders live precisely in the positions that feel safe.
- Checking your candidate move's IDEA but not the opponent's FORCING replies to it.
- Playing instantly after the opponent's "obvious" move — obvious moves carry hidden threats too.

**How to think:** Institute the three-question gate before every move release: (1) What are ALL their checks in reply? (2) All their captures? (3) What did my move stop defending or block? Ten seconds. In time trouble, compress to one question: "does this move hang anything to a check or capture?" The gate must survive time pressure or it isn't a habit yet.

**Example:** You're winning a rook up and play the natural Qd4 centralizing. Gate question 1: their checks... Re1-e8 is check — and mate. The gate exists exactly for this: winning positions kill vigilance, and one skipped scan converts +5 into 0-1.

**Coach's tip:** After every loss, identify the losing move and write down which gate question would have caught it. Nearly all blunders fail question 1 or 2 — proof the routine works when actually run.

**Train it:** Play three slow games with a physical ritual (touch the clock zone, then scan) before each move. Habit formation needs a body anchor, not just intention.
