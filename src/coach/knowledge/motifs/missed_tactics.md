# Missed Tactics

A missed tactic is a winning shot — material or mate — that was available on your move and not played. Engines tag these mercilessly; coaches care about WHY they were invisible.

**Why it matters:** At most levels you get two or three winning tactical moments per game. Converting even one more of them per game changes results faster than any other improvement.

**Recognize it:** The moments tactics hide: right after the opponent's piece moved away from a duty (what did it stop defending?), right after any check becomes available, when enemy pieces align (fork/skewer geometry), and — the classic — the move right after you thought "I'm just better here."

**Common mistakes:**
- Scanning for tactics only when the position "feels tactical" — the biggest shots hide in quiet positions where nobody looks.
- Calculating the flashy candidate and skipping the boring capture that simply wins a pawn.
- Finding the idea, rejecting it on a ghost ("...something recaptures") without actually calculating the refutation.

**How to think:** Give every position a five-second CCT sweep (checks, captures, threats) BEFORE strategic thinking — not after. When a sweep item "almost works," don't drop it: name the one defender or resource that stops it, and check whether a preparatory move removes it. Half of all missed wins were seen and wrongly discarded.

**Example:** Opponent plays Rd8-d5 "actively." The sweep asks what the rook abandoned: the d8-square... and your Nf7+ family of forks now comes with Rxd8 follow-ups. Tactics rarely announce themselves; they leak in through squares the last move stopped watching.

**Coach's tip:** Review every analyzed game's missed tactics and sort them into three buckets: never looked, looked but miscalculated, saw idea but chickened out. Each bucket has a different cure — know which one is yours.

**Train it:** Do puzzle sets where some positions have NO tactic (mixed sets) — the skill isn't solving, it's detecting. Ten minutes daily beats two hours weekly.
