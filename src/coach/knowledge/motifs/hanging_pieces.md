# Hanging Pieces

A hanging piece is one that can be captured for free or for insufficient compensation — undefended, or defended fewer times than it is attacked.

**Why it matters:** Below 1800, more games are decided by hung pieces than by every other factor combined. Fixing this one leak is worth more rating than any opening study.

**Recognize it:** After every move (yours and theirs), two scans: which of MY pieces are now undefended or newly attacked? What did my last move STOP defending? Loose pieces — even currently unattacked ones — are accidents waiting: "loose pieces drop off" to forks and double attacks.

**Common mistakes:**
- Moving a defender without noticing what it defended (the invisible error behind most hangs).
- Counting attackers and defenders but ignoring ORDER and VALUE: a pawn defended by a queen against rook+bishop attackers still falls profitably.
- Tunnel vision during your own attack — the moment you smell a king hunt is the moment your queenside starts hanging.

**How to think:** Build the end-of-move ritual: before releasing a move, name (1) every opponent check, (2) every capture they have, (3) what your move left behind. Three seconds, every move, no exceptions — consistency beats brilliance here.

**Example:** You play Nc3-d5 attacking a queen. Strong move? The ritual asks what the knight LEFT: it was blocking the c-file, and your c2-pawn — with your king behind it — is now hit by their rook. The best players see what a move abandons as vividly as what it attacks.

**Coach's tip:** Track your "free piece" losses for ten games — count them honestly. Most players discover one specific trigger (time trouble, attacking excitement, recaptures) and can fix the trigger rather than the whole habit.

**Train it:** Play five slow games where you write a checkmark on your scoresheet after performing the blunder-check ritual each move. The physical act builds the mental habit.
