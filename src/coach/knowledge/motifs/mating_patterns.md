# Mating Patterns

Mating patterns are the standard checkmate geometries — back-rank, smothered, Greek gift, Anastasia's, Arabian, Boden's. Knowing them converts long calculations into instant recognition.

**Why it matters:** You cannot calculate toward a target you can't picture. Players who know thirty patterns "see" mates in seconds that pattern-blind players never consider at all.

**Recognize it:** An attack becomes a mating attack when three things align: an exposed or boxed-in king, more attackers than defenders in the king's zone, and open lines (files, diagonals, or knight routes) into that zone. When all three exist, a named pattern is usually available — go pattern-hunting, not move-hunting.

**Common mistakes:**
- Collecting material in a winning attack instead of finishing (every extra move lets defenders regroup).
- Starting a sacrifice-fueled attack with only two attackers — most patterns need three pieces' worth of force.
- Ignoring defensive pattern knowledge: knowing Greek-gift conditions tells you when h7 is actually safe.

**How to think:** In attacking positions, ask "which mate am I building?" and name it. Then work backward: what must be true (queen reaches h-file, knight lands on f6/g5, rook lift available)? Each requirement becomes a candidate move. Checks, captures, threats — in that order — assemble the picture.

**Example:** Smothered mate: 1.Nf7+ (king must go to g8 corner) Kg8 2.Nh6+ double check! Kh8 (2...Kf8 3.Qf7#) 3.Qg8+!! Rxg8 4.Nf7# — queen decoy plus knight geometry. Once seen, never unseen: you will now find this in real games for the rest of your life.

**Coach's tip:** Learn patterns in pairs: the mate AND its minimum conditions. "Bxh7+ works when Ng5+ follows and the queen reaches h5" is knowledge; "sac on h7" alone is a donation.

**Train it:** Drill one pattern family per week (10 puzzles each): back-rank, smothered, Greek gift, Arabian/Anastasia, Boden's. Five weeks buys a lifetime of one-second recognitions.
