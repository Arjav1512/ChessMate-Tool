# Development

Development means bringing your pieces from their starting squares to squares where they influence the game. A developed army fights; an undeveloped one watches.

**Why it matters:** Tempo is the currency of the opening. Each wasted move is a free move for your opponent, and three free moves is usually enough to launch a winning attack in an open position.

**Recognize it:** Count pieces off their home squares after move 8–10. A lead of two or more pieces is a real advantage — if you have it, look for ways to open the position; if you are behind, avoid opening lines until you catch up.

**Common mistakes:**
- Developing the queen early where it becomes a target ("develop with tempo" works against you).
- Making three pawn moves to win one pawn while the king sits in the center.
- Developing pieces to squares where they block your own pawns or bishops (the classic ...Bd6 blocking the d-pawn... wait for the pawn first).

**How to think:** Knights before bishops is a guideline, not a law — the real rule is: develop toward the center, create a threat if you can, and never block your own development. Ask "which piece is doing the least?" and improve that one.

**Example:** In the Scandinavian after 1.e4 d5 2.exd5 Qxd5 3.Nc3, White develops a knight *and* gains a tempo on the queen. Black's early queen sortie hands White a free developing move.

**Coach's tip:** When you cannot decide between two moves, pick the one that develops a new piece. It is right often enough to be your default.

**Train it:** Review three of your recent losses. Write down how many pieces each side had developed on move 10 — you will usually find the story of the game right there.
