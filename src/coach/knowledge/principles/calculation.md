# Calculation

Calculation is seeing forced sequences accurately before playing them. Where judgment says "this looks good," calculation proves it — and in forcing positions, proof is the only thing that counts.

**Why it matters:** One concrete line outweighs any amount of general reasoning. Most blunders are not knowledge failures; they are calculation failures — a missed reply, a stopped-too-early line, a move-order slip.

**Recognize it:** The position demands calculation when checks, captures, or direct threats exist for either side, when material is hanging, or when you are about to enter any forcing sequence (especially trades into endgames).

**Common mistakes:**
- Calculating your ideas deeply but your opponent's replies shallowly.
- Stopping a line one move too early — right where the tactic hides.
- Recalculating the same pretty line five times while never checking the ugly refutation once.

**How to think:** Use the forcing-moves ladder: list every check, then every capture, then every threat — for BOTH sides — before evaluating quiet moves. Calculate one candidate to a quiet position, evaluate, then move to the next candidate. Never hop between lines half-finished.

**Example:** You see 1.Nxe5 winning a pawn because 1...Nxe5 2.Qxd8. The blunder-check asks: what else recaptures? 1...Bxf2+! first — a zwischenzug — and the "free pawn" loses a piece. The ladder catches it because the check gets listed before you commit.

**Coach's tip:** Before releasing any move, run the ten-second blunder check: every opponent check, every capture, every one-move threat in reply. Ten seconds per move prevents half of all lost games.

**Train it:** Solve tactics with a strict rule — write down the FULL line including the opponent's best defense before moving a piece. Accuracy first, speed later.
