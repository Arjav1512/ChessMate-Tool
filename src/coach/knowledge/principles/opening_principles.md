# Opening Principles

The opening has one job: reach a middlegame where your king is safe, your pieces are active, and you own a share of the center. It is not about memorizing moves — it is about earning the right to play a good middlegame.

**Why it matters:** Most games below master level are decided by opening sins that surface ten moves later: an uncastled king gets attacked, an undeveloped queenside never joins the game, a grabbed pawn costs three tempi.

**Recognize it:** You are violating principles when you move the same piece twice before move 8 without a concrete reason, when your queen is the first piece out, or when you count more pawn moves than piece moves on your scoresheet.

**Common mistakes:**
- Chasing material or "free" pawns while behind in development.
- Copying memorized moves into positions where they no longer fit.
- Delaying castling "one more move" until the tactics arrive.

**How to think:** Each move, ask three questions in order: Is my king getting safer? Am I developing a new piece toward the center? Am I fighting for the central squares? A move that answers none of these needs a concrete tactical justification.

**Example:** After 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.Ng5 — White moves a developed knight twice to threaten f7. It works only because the threat is concrete (5.Nxf7). Without such a point, 4.Ng5 would just be a wasted tempo.

**Coach's tip:** Development is a race you can lose invisibly. Count developed pieces after move 10 — if your opponent has two more, you are already defending.

**Train it:** Play five games where you may not move any piece twice before move 10 unless a tactic forces it. Notice how naturally strong your positions become.
