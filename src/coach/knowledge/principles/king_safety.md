# King Safety

Most decisive attacks trace back to one of two causes: a king left in the center too long, or a castled king whose pawn shield was weakened without need.

**Why it matters:** Material and positional advantages mean nothing if you get mated first. Games are lost in one move near the king that would take twenty moves to lose anywhere else.

**Recognize it:** Danger signs — the center is opening and your king hasn't castled; you have pushed a pawn in front of your castled king; the defenders around your king are outnumbered by nearby attackers; an open file or diagonal points at your king.

**Common mistakes:**
- Delaying castling to win material, then never getting to castle at all.
- Playing h3/h6 automatically "for luft" — every shield move creates a hook for a pawn storm (…g5–g4 hits h3).
- Trading your only defensive minor piece (the f3-knight or fianchettoed bishop) and only then noticing the holes it guarded.

**How to think:** Evaluate king safety by counting: attackers that can reach the king's zone within two moves versus defenders already there. If attackers lead, defend *before* the threats become forcing — one prophylactic move early beats three desperate ones late.

**Example:** The Greek gift Bxh7+ works when three conditions meet: the bishop can reach h7, a knight can follow with Ng5+, and the queen joins via h5 or d3. If you count those attackers before weakening h7's protection, you never fall for it.

**Coach's tip:** Before every pawn move in front of your king, name the concrete gain. "It felt active" is how attacks against you are born.

**Train it:** Set up ten mating attacks from a tactics book and identify, in each, which earlier pawn move or trade made the attack possible.
