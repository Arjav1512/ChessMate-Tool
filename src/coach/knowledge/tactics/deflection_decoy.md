# Deflection and Decoys

Deflection drags a piece AWAY from a duty; a decoy lures a piece TO a fatal square. Both manipulate the opponent's pieces onto losing geometry — usually with a sacrifice they can't decline.

**Why it matters:** These are the themes behind most queen sacrifices and mating combinations. Recognizing them turns "wow" moves into calculable, repeatable technique.

**Recognize it:** Deflection candidates: an enemy piece with a critical job (guarding a mate square, a back rank, a promotion square). Decoy candidates: a fork, skewer, or mate that would work "if only the king/queen stood THERE." The sacrifice is the taxi that takes them there.

**Common mistakes:**
- Checking whether the sacrifice can be taken, but not whether it can be DECLINED — a good deflection must hurt either way.
- Deflecting a piece that has a second route back to its duty.
- Using a decoy to win material while allowing counterplay: forced sequences first, collections second.

**How to think:** Start from the dream position ("if the queen weren't on d8, Re8 would mate"), then ask what forcing move makes the dream real: a capture the queen must answer, a check the king must walk into. Verify both branches: acceptance AND the best decline.

**Example:** Classic back-rank deflection: your rook on e1 stares at e8, and only Black's queen on d8 prevents Re8#. Attack her with 1.Qa5! — a piece she cannot capture (1...Qxa5?? 2.Re8#) and cannot ignore. Whatever she does, she remains chained to e8's defense while you collect on the other wing. One overloaded piece, two duties, zero good moves.

**Coach's tip:** When a combination almost works, don't discard it — write down WHICH enemy piece refutes it. That piece is your deflection target; the tactic is one preparatory move away.

**Train it:** Solve 15 deflection/decoy puzzles; for each, state the overloaded piece's two duties in words before moving.
