# Zwischenzug (In-Between Move)

A zwischenzug interrupts an "obvious" forced sequence with a stronger intermediate move — usually a check or bigger threat — before making the expected recapture.

**Why it matters:** Assumed recaptures are where calculation silently breaks. Most deep tactical errors aren't missed moves; they're moves inserted by the opponent between YOUR moves.

**Recognize it:** Any time a sequence includes the thought "and then they must retake," stop: must they? Checks, mate threats, and captures of bigger targets all outrank a recapture. Scan for them — yours and theirs — at every half-move of a forcing line.

**Common mistakes:**
- Calculating trades as if recaptures were laws of physics.
- Making YOUR recapture instantly when a zwischenzug was available ("check before you retake").
- Playing an in-between move that gives up more than it gains — the interruption must be genuinely stronger, not just clever.

**How to think:** Adopt the rule: in every forcing line, before writing the opponent's "forced" reply, list their checks and captures instead. If any of them creates a bigger problem than the material they left hanging, your line is broken. Do the same for yourself — free zwischenzugs are lying around in your own games.

**Example:** 1.d4 Nf6 2.Bg5 Ne4?! 3.Bf4 c5 4.dxc5?? — White assumes ...Nxc5 or ...e6 next. Instead 4...Qa5+! (zwischenzug) picks up the c5-pawn AND keeps the monster e4-knight: 5.c3 Qxc5. The check outranked every "expected" move.

**Coach's tip:** The three most profitable words in calculation: "instead of retaking." Say them once per forcing line and your rating will thank you.

**Train it:** Solve 15 zwischenzug-tagged puzzles. Then review two of your recent games hunting for in-between moves both sides missed — there is almost always one.
