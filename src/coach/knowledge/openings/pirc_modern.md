# Pirc and Modern Defense (1.e4 d6 / 1.e4 g6)

The Pirc (with ...Nf6) and Modern (without) let White build a full pawn center, then attack it with the g7-bishop and well-timed pawn strikes — hypermodern chess in its purest form.

**Why it matters:** These defenses teach the deepest counterattacking lesson: a big center is a target, not (only) an asset. They also reward players who prefer understanding to memorization.

**Recognize it:** Black's scheme: ...d6, ...Nf6 (Pirc), ...g6, ...Bg7, ...O-O, then a central strike with ...e5 or ...c5. White chooses aggression (Austrian Attack: f4) or restraint (Classical: Nf3, Be2).

**Common mistakes:**
- As Black, delaying the central strike: without a timely ...e5 or ...c5 the position simply becomes a bad, cramped one.
- Trading the g7-bishop or letting it bite on granite behind ...e5 with the center locked wrong.
- As White, pushing every pawn forward (e4, d4, f4, e5, f5...) until the overextended chain collapses to one precise counter.

**How to think:** Black plays a waiting game with a tripwire: develop inside three ranks, watch White commit, then strike where the center is least defended. The evaluation question is always: can White's space become an attack before Black's counter lands?

**Example:** Austrian Attack: 1.e4 d6 2.d4 Nf6 3.Nc3 g6 4.f4 Bg7 5.Nf3 O-O 6.Bd3 Nc6! — pressuring d4 immediately; after 7.e5 dxe5 8.fxe5 Nh5 Black's pieces swarm the loosened dark squares. One tempo of White carelessness and the big center becomes a big liability.

**Coach's tip:** Playing the Pirc is a promise to counterattack. If you castle and then shuffle, you've broken the promise and will simply be squeezed.

**Train it:** From the Austrian tabiya, find Black's best strike (...c5, ...e5, or ...Nc6) against three different White 6th moves. Check with an engine only after committing.
