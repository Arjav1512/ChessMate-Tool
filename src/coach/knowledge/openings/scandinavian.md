# Scandinavian Defense (1.e4 d5)

The Scandinavian challenges e4 immediately and accepts an early queen development after 2.exd5 Qxd5 — trading a tempo for a simple, solid structure with no theoretical minefields.

**Why it matters:** For club players it delivers the same position type every game (a Caro-Kann-like wall) with minimal memorization. Its clear piece placement scheme teaches setup-thinking.

**Recognize it:** Main line: 2.exd5 Qxd5 3.Nc3 Qa5 (or the modern 3...Qd6) 4.d4 Nf6 5.Nf3 c6 6.Bc4 Bf5 — Black builds c6/e6 pawns with both bishops OUTSIDE the wall, then castles either side.

**Common mistakes:**
- As Black, wandering with the queen a third and fourth time — one tempo was the deal; more and White's lead becomes an attack.
- Playing ...e6 before ...Bf5/...Bg4, locking in the light-squared bishop (the whole point was to avoid that).
- As White, chasing the queen with pawn moves that loosen the position instead of developing with the tempo gained.

**How to think:** Black plays for structure: the c6/e6 wall is nearly unbreakable, so White must attack before Black completes development — usually with d4–d5 breaks or piece pressure on f7/b7. As Black, count development: if White reaches a 3-piece lead, close lines and castle fast.

**Example:** After 3...Qa5 4.d4 Nf6 5.Nf3 Bf5?! 6.Bd2! already threatens Nd5 discovered-attack tricks against the a5-queen — the standard punishment geometry every Scandinavian player must know (…c6 first prevents it).

**Coach's tip:** The a5-queen and c7-pawn sit on the same diagonal as White's dark-squared bishop. Before every routine move as Black, check the Nd5/Bb4 discovered-attack motifs.

**Train it:** Drill the first ten moves of the 3...Qa5 main line until automatic, then play it in blitz — the Scandinavian rewards knowing your scheme cold.
