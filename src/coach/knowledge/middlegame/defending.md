# Defending

Defense is active problem-solving under pressure: trading attackers, refusing weaknesses, timing the counterstrike. Good defenders don't survive attacks — they outlive them with a better position.

**Why it matters:** You will be worse in half your games; what happens next is the biggest rating separator there is. Attacks fail against precise defense far more often than they succeed — someone just has to play the defense.

**Recognize it:** Real danger versus noise: count effective attackers versus defenders near your king, and check whether the attack has an actual line-opening lever. Three attackers with no lever is theater; two attackers with an unstoppable h5-h6 is an emergency.

**Common mistakes:**
- Panicking materially — giving up pawns or the exchange to "stop the attack" when a cold defensive move held everything.
- Pure passivity: defending every threat one move at a time until the threats stack faster than the answers ("passive defense is slow death").
- Weakening pawn moves under pressure (...h6, ...g6, f3) — attackers pray for exactly these hooks.

**How to think:** The defender's toolbox, in preference order: (1) trade attackers — especially their most dangerous piece — every trade halves the attack; (2) refuse to move king-zone pawns without necessity; (3) return material to break the attack's momentum at the right moment; (4) counterattack — the center first ("a flank attack is answered in the center"); (5) run the king early, not late. Every defensive move should also do something constructive if possible.

**Example:** Facing the Greek gift: after Bxh7+ Kxh7 Ng5+, the difference between ...Kg8?? (mate soon) and ...Kg6! (the fighting square) is preparation: knowing the defensive pattern in advance. Most "brilliant" attacks have a known defense — defenders who study attacks own them both.

**Coach's tip:** When defending, your pieces' morale is your morale. One active counter-threat — even a small one — changes the game's psychology and splits the attacker's attention.

**Train it:** Set an engine to attack you from five classic attacking positions (defender's side). Losing these repeatedly, then holding them, builds real confidence no amount of winning teaches.
