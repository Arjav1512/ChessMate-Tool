# Attacking the King

A king attack is a numbers game played with force, lines, and time: more attackers than defenders, open routes to the king, and threats fast enough to outrun counterplay.

**Why it matters:** Mating attacks end games immediately — no technique phase, no endgame risk. Every player needs attacking skill; even pure positional players convert their advantages through a final attack more often than not.

**Recognize it:** Attack-readiness checklist: (1) attackers vs defenders in the king zone — you need superiority, usually 3+ effective pieces; (2) open or openable lines toward the king; (3) a stable enough center (flank attacks die to central counters); (4) the king can't run cheaply.

**Common mistakes:**
- Attacking with two pieces and hoping — sacrifices need force behind them, not optimism.
- Pawn-storming in front of your OWN king when castled same-side.
- Winning the attack but not the game: cashing into material while mate was available, or checking aimlessly until the king walks to safety ("checks are questions — only give ones with good answers").

**How to think:** Attack in phases: accumulate (aim pieces at the zone, provoke weaknesses with threats), open (the pawn lever or sacrifice that rips lines — h4-h5, g5-g6, Bxh7), invade (heavy pieces enter through the opened lines), and only then calculate mate. Each phase completes before the next begins; most failed attacks skipped a phase.

**Example:** Opposite-side castling recipe: White O-O-O, Black O-O. White plays h4-h5xg6 while Black plays ...b5-b4xc3. Both attacks are "correct" — the race is decided by tempi: every non-attacking move is a half-point donated. Count both sides' moves-to-breakthrough before choosing any quiet move.

**Coach's tip:** Before sacrificing, count the defenders you'll face AFTER the sacrifice, not before it. Many "unsound" attacks fail because one defensive trade was available mid-sequence.

**Train it:** Study five classic same-side attacking games (Greek gift and h-file themes), listing for each: which weakness invited the attack, which lever opened it, which piece delivered mate.
