# Middlegame Planning

A plan is a short chain of achievable goals rooted in the position's features — not a wish ("attack the king") but a route (open the h-file, trade the f6-defender, double heavy pieces).

**Why it matters:** Between opening knowledge and endgame technique lies the phase that decides most games — and the phase where "I didn't know what to do" costs players hundreds of rating points. A modest plan beats brilliant planlessness.

**Recognize it:** Plan-forming moments: the last book move, any structure change (trade, break, lock), and every time you catch yourself shuffling. If you cannot state your current plan in one sentence, you don't have one.

**Common mistakes:**
- Planning from fantasy instead of features — the structure, worst piece, and pawn breaks TELL you the plan; ignoring them to "attack" fails against any defense.
- Abandoning a sound plan after one surprising reply (re-check the plan, then usually continue).
- Making one-move plans: threat, parried, new unrelated threat, parried — accumulating nothing.

**How to think:** Build plans from the position's three questions: Where is my pawn-break lever? Which is my worst piece and its dream square? Which side of the board does my structure point toward? A real plan usually reads like: "reroute the b1-knight via d2 and f3 to e5, prepare f4-f5, keep their ...d5 break impossible." Include the opponent's counter-plan and your answer to it.

**Example:** Carlsbad structure, White: the features say queenside (b4-b5 minority attack), the worst piece says Bd3 wants f5's diagonal after... every move for fifteen moves serves b5 or prevents ...Ne4. Games like these feel "effortless" — that's what a feature-based plan does.

**Coach's tip:** Write your plan (one sentence) on your scoresheet at move 15 in training games. If you can't, that's the real problem to fix — not your tactics.

**Train it:** Take five master games, cover the moves at move 14, and write the plan you'd choose for the winner. Compare with what they played — you're training the exact skill.
